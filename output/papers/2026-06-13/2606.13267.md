# TimeLens: On-Device Artifact Recognition with Retrieval-Augmented Question Answering for the Grand Egyptian Museum

- **Authors:** Rawan Hesham, Ali Ashraf, Amr Ahmed, Malak Alaa, Omar Ahmed, Omar Wagih
- **Published:** 2026-06-11
- **Categories:** cs.CV, cs.CL, cs.IR
- **arXiv:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13267v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13267v1)
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13267v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13267v1)

## Abstract

TimeLens is an AI-powered bilingual mobile guide for the Grand Egyptian Museum (GEM). Pointing a phone at an exhibit, a visitor sees the artifact recognized in real time and can ask follow-up questions answered in English or Arabic. The work addresses three problems specific to in-gallery deployment: fine-grained visual similarity among 51 catalogued artifacts (many near-identical Ramesside statues), the gap between curated training data and handheld camera conditions, and the risk of an AI guide stating unsupported historical facts. Two engineering contributions are reported. First, an on-device artifact detector was developed through a data-quality-driven iteration study -- from foundation-model auto-annotation (YOLO-World), through spatial label-cleaning rules, to a fully hand-annotated dataset -- isolating label quality as the decisive factor: the final YOLOv8n model resolves every previously failing class while remaining a 5.97 MB TensorFlow Lite asset that runs in real time on a mid-range phone (mAP@0.5 = 0.995, mAP@0.5:0.95 = 0.924). Second, a bilingual Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) guide, grounded in a 108-record ChromaDB knowledge base, was benchmarked across seven candidate language models, with Gemma 4 E2B (Q4 K M) selected; ten targeted optimizations reduce end-to-end latency from over 30 s to approximately 10 s. Both subsystems are integrated in a production Flutter application with bilingual interface, museum location gating, and text-to-speech support.

## Research content

TimeLens is an AI-powered bilingual mobile guide for the Grand Egyptian Museum that enables real-time artifact recognition and follow-up question answering in English or Arabic. The system consists of two main components: (1) an on-device artifact detector using YOLOv8n (5.97 MB TensorFlow Lite model) that achieves mAP@0.5 = 0.995 and mAP@0.5:0.95 = 0.924, developed through a data-quality-driven iteration from foundation-model auto-annotation to hand-annotated dataset; and (2) a bilingual Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) guide using Gemma 4 E2B (Q4 K M) with a 108-record ChromaDB knowledge base, optimized from over 30 s to approximately 10 s end-to-end latency. Both subsystems are integrated in a production Flutter application with bilingual interface, museum location gating, and text-to-speech support.

## Problem addressed

Three problems specific to in-gallery deployment: (1) fine-grained visual similarity among 51 catalogued artifacts (many near-identical Ramesside statues), (2) the gap between curated training data and handheld camera conditions, and (3) the risk of an AI guide stating unsupported historical facts.

## Limitations

- The RAG guide's end-to-end latency of approximately 10 s, while improved from over 30 s, may still impact user experience for real-time interaction
- The knowledge base is limited to 108 records, which may constrain the breadth of historical information available
- The system is specifically designed for 51 catalogued artifacts, limiting scalability to larger collections without additional data collection and annotation
- The artifact detector was evaluated on a mid-range phone; performance on lower-end devices is not reported
- The evaluation focuses on technical metrics (mAP, latency) rather than user studies or deployment metrics in actual museum conditions

## Possible improvement directions

- Further reduce the RAG end-to-end latency below 10 seconds through additional optimizations or hardware acceleration
- Expand the knowledge base beyond 108 records to cover more artifacts and historical depth
- Evaluate and optimize performance on a wider range of device tiers, including lower-end smartphones
- Conduct user studies to assess visitor experience and information retention in actual museum deployment
- Extend the artifact catalog beyond the initial 51 artifacts to scale coverage of the museum collection
- Explore additional model compression techniques to further reduce the detector size while maintaining accuracy
