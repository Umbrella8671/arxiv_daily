# Substrate Asymmetry in User-Side Memory: A Diagnostic Framework

- **Authors:** Youwang Deng
- **Published:** 2026-06-10
- **Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG
- **arXiv:** [http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11712v1](http://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11712v1)
- **PDF:** [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11712v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11712v1)

## Abstract

User-side memory in LLMs is typically scored as a single "personalization" capability: given a user's history, is the output more user-aware? We show this aggregate metric hides opposite-direction failures. Memory factorises into at least three orthogonal axes -- behavioral consistency (style, voice), factual presence (recall facts in history), and factual absence (abstain when a fact is absent) -- and no single substrate wins all three. Comparing per-user gamma-LoRA (a small LoRA adapter trained on each user's history; gamma denotes per-user, not per-task) against BGE-large dense top-K retrieval on a controlled 50-user synthetic corpus and a real-data probe (LaMP-3), we find gamma-LoRA decisively wins behavioral style while RAG decisively wins factual absence -- and the same query-projection cells in attention layers 21-35 causally load-bear both effects in opposite directions (zeroing those LoRA weights raises absence-probe TPR by +33 pp and drops presence-probe TPR by 20 pp). On the more heavily RLHF-tuned Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct the asymmetry strengthens, not heals: parametric memory's behavioral advantage collapses while its absence-calibration deficit against retrieval widens -- an alignment tax on parametric user-memory. On real-data LaMP-3, gamma-LoRA underperforms a majority baseline; a 9-condition mitigation sweep diagnoses this as instruction-following collapse, not substrate failure (a 9x2 cross-product shows the eval-time {1..5} logit mask drives main_acc to >=0.995 on every recipe), and the best training-time fix replicates bit-identically on Llama. Finally, substrate-selection routing is question-classification, not calibration: a 110M DistilBERT on the question text alone beats every logit-based router. We contribute the diagnostic framework, the diagnosed real-data negative, the alignment-tax replication, and the routing-as-classification finding.

## Research content

The paper introduces a diagnostic framework for analyzing user-side memory in LLMs, decomposing it into three orthogonal axes: behavioral consistency (style, voice), factual presence (recall facts in history), and factual absence (abstain when a fact is absent). The authors compare per-user gamma-LoRA (small LoRA adapters trained on each user's history) against BGE-large dense top-K retrieval on a controlled 50-user synthetic corpus and real-data LaMP-3. They identify that gamma-LoRA wins on behavioral style while RAG wins on factual absence, with the same query-projection cells in attention layers 21-35 causally mediating both effects in opposite directions. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the asymmetry strengthens with more RLHF tuning, showing an alignment tax on parametric user-memory. For LaMP-3, gamma-LoRA underperforms a majority baseline due to instruction-following collapse, which is fixable with eval-time logit masking. ## Problem addressed

User-side memory in LLMs is typically evaluated as a single aggregate 'personalization' metric, which obscures opposite-direction failures across different dimensions of memory. The paper addresses this lack of diagnostic granularity and investigates whether a single substrate can excel across all memory axes.

## Limitations

- No single substrate wins across all three memory axes (behavioral consistency, factual presence, factual absence)
- On more heavily RLHF-tuned models (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), parametric memory's behavioral advantage collapses while its absence-calibration deficit against retrieval widens
- On real-data LaMP-3, gamma-LoRA underperforms a majority baseline due to instruction-following collapse

## Possible improvement directions

- Eval-time {1..5} logit mask can drive main_acc to >=0.995 on every recipe, fixing the instruction-following collapse
- Training-time fixes that replicate bit-identically on Llama for the LaMP-3 underperformance
- Substrate-selection routing via question-classification (110M DistilBERT on question text alone) rather than logit-based calibration approaches
